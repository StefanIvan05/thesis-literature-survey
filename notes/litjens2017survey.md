# Litjens et al., 2017, Medical Image Analysis

- **BibTeX key:** `litjens2017survey`
- **Category:** Overview
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2017.07.005
- **PDF:** [`papers/litjens2017survey.pdf`](../papers/litjens2017survey.pdf)

## Summary
Survey of more than 300 papers applying deep learning to medical images, organised by task (classification, detection, segmentation, registration) and by anatomy (brain, eye, chest, breast, skin, etc.). Concludes that CNNs became the dominant method and that the main bottleneck is the availability of large, well-annotated datasets.

## Why it is relevant to my thesis
Gives the overall map of the field and the standard terminology (classification vs. detection vs. segmentation). Its per-anatomy sections show which organs/modalities are well studied, which helps me choose a specific condition.

## Where I will use it
Introduction and Related Work chapters; justification that dataset quality is the key constraint.

## Limitations / open questions
Published in 2017, so it predates Vision Transformers, nnU-Net and foundation models; I need newer sources for the current state of the art.
